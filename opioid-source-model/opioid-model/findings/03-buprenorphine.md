Capacity limits treatment only while demand outgrows it. As heroin addiction falls, demand eases, so raising each prescriber's capacity saves lives in the first years and then stops mattering, and adding prescribers never matters.

> Increasing the number of buprenorphine providers does not reduce overdose deaths in the near or long term. Growing buprenorphine providers' capacity to treat more people, on the other hand, has among the largest effects on opioid overdose deaths in the near term, reaching a peak annual reduction of approximately 2% by mid-2023 before falling quickly to having no impact relative to baseline.

## Runs

- **No policy**: Trends continue, no policy change.
- **20% more prescribers** (policy BupProv): More providers who may legally prescribe buprenorphine (waivers).
- **20% more patients per prescriber** (policy BupProvBarriers): Existing providers can each treat more patients.

## Paper

More prescribers: no effect. More patients per prescriber (20%): about 2% fewer deaths at the peak in mid-2023, then none.
