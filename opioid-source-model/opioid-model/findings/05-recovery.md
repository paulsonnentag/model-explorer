Relapse after remission is becoming the main source of addiction. Keeping people in remission therefore does more than preventing new cases, and peer recovery, where more people in remission lower everyone's relapse rate, grows its own effect.

> The recovery support strategies … are the most impactful in reducing OUD prevalence by 2032 (−5.2% for return to OUD and −5.4% for peer recovery) … The peer recovery loop has an even larger impact than the direct reduction in the return to OUD rate because of the loop's reinforcing nature.

## Runs

- **No policy**: Trends continue, no policy change.
- **20% fewer relapses** (policy ReturntoOUD): A lower rate of returning to addiction after remission.
- **Peer recovery support** (policy PeerRecovery): More people in remission lower the relapse rate; set to a 20% cut by 2025.

## Paper

People with addiction 5.2% (fewer relapses) and 5.4% (peer recovery) lower by 2032; deaths 5.7% and 6.1% lower in 2032.

## Caveat

Peer recovery is the one policy that adds a loop: it switches on 'People in recovery affect relapse', which the fitted model keeps at zero for policy testing.
