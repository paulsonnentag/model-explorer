Fewer prescriptions mean fewer pills to misuse, so pill addiction falls. But some people who would have misused pills switch to heroin, so heroin addiction first rises above the no-policy run, and only falls below it once fewer people are addicted to pills and could switch.

> The effect of reducing opioid prescribing on OUD involving heroin exhibits a worse-before-better dynamic. At first, it increases slightly compared to baseline because reducing prescription opioid availability leads some people to switch to heroin … However, starting around 2028, OUD involving heroin falls compared to baseline.

## Runs

- **No policy**: Trends continue, no policy change.
- **20% fewer patients prescribed** (policy RxRate): Fewer people receive an opioid prescription.
- **50% fewer patients prescribed** (policy RxRate 50): The same policy at the paper's largest effect size.
