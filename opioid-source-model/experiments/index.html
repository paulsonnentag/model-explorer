FDA SOURCE opioid model: visualization experiments

Different ways to look at the same model: Lim et al. 2022 (PNAS), the .mdl file, its data and the published runs. Every experiment runs the model live in the browser on the same engine, which is checked against Vensim's published results.

  1. 01

    Explorer

    A dependency graph centred on one variable at a time: where it comes from, what it drives, and what it means. Edit any assumption and every chart re-runs. Switch to the modelers' own stock-and-flow diagrams, running live, and see where people come from and go for any group.

    Question: what is this number made of?
  2. 02

    Paper view

    The PDF on the left. Click a figure, the table or a highlighted claim, and the right side opens it up: the variables it plots, the data behind it, the assumptions it depends on, and a live re-run.

    Question: where does this figure come from, and does it hold up?
  3. 03

    Model trail

    Start from a paper, a finding, the feedback loops, the stock and flow map or the what-ifs, and follow links to the right: each opens a panel, or scrolls to it if it is already open. All panels show one run of the model, and whatever the pointer is over lights up on the map. Everything is drawn from opioid-model/, the authors' package sorted by purpose: the model as authored in one model.mdl, what-if files, data, the papers, and our findings as Markdown. Every panel links back to the file and line it comes from.

    Question: where does each claim come from in the model, and what in the model makes it so?
  4. 04

    Diff review

    A change to the model reviewed like a pull request: the one fix the authors made after publishing, and four proposed changes built from their own files (a loop knockout, a policy, a test, a new flow), picked from the header. Next to the raw diff, a model-aware view keeps what changes the runs and folds what Vensim rewrites on every save. The stock and flow map, the feedback loops and the results open alongside, with the run before the change dashed and what the change acts on marked; the authors' published numbers sit next to ours.

    Question: what does this change actually do to the model and its results?
