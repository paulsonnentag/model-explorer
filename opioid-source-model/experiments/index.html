FDA SOURCE opioid model: visualization experiments

Different ways to look at the same model: Lim et al. 2022 (PNAS), the .mdl file, its data and the published runs. Every experiment runs the model live in the browser on the same engine, which is checked against Vensim's published results.

  1. 01

    Explorer

    A dependency graph centred on one variable at a time: where it comes from, what it drives, and what it means. Edit any assumption and every chart re-runs. Switch to the modelers' own stock-and-flow diagrams, running live, and see where people come from and go for any group.

    Question: what is this number made of?
  2. 02

    Paper view

    The PDF on the left. Click a figure, the table or a highlighted claim, and the right side opens it up: the variables it plots, the data behind it, the assumptions it depends on, and a live re-run.

    Question: where does this figure come from, and does it hold up?
  3. 03

    Stock map

    All 14 groups of people on one page, laid out by rule (stage of addiction × which opioids) rather than drawn by hand. Arrows are as thick as the flow of people; press play to watch the epidemic move through the system. The effects found in the equations are listed beside it as formulas; open one to paint the flows it changes. Stocks and flows unfold in place into their history and the factors of their rate. Five findings from the two SOURCE papers sit on the left, each with the model's charts against historical data for every alternative (without fentanyl, another future, or one of the authors' policies); pick one to run the whole map in that state.

    Question: where are people, and where are they going?
