SOURCE: a model of the US opioid crisis

The FDA needed to know how the opioid crisis would evolve and how policies would play out, given long delays and feedback loops between policy and behaviour. SOURCE tracks the US population aged 12+ as people move between misusing prescription opioids, using heroin and fentanyl, addiction, treatment, remission, and overdose death, from 1999 to 2032.

What the model answered

  • Naloxone handed out to laypeople averted about 19,800 deaths (1999–2020).
  • Without illicit fentanyl there would have been about 59,000 fewer deaths, much less than the 228,000 fentanyl-involved deaths recorded, because fentanyl deaths also scared people away from starting heroin.
  • Deaths are projected to peak and then decline, with 543,000–842,000 total deaths in 2020–2032 depending on the scenario.

Feedback loops

What makes the model dynamic: transition rates respond to the state of the crisis instead of being fixed.

Social influence feedback

Reinforcing loop: the more people use a drug, the more others start (and the faster addiction develops).

47 variables · 11-social-influence-feedback.txt

Perceived risk feedback

Balancing loop: overdose deaths make a drug seem riskier, so fewer people start. This is what eventually bends the curve.

32 variables · 12-perceived-risk-feedback.txt

Rx and Heroin availability feedback

Feedback loop: availability of prescription opioids and heroin affects how many people start.

27 variables · 10-rx-and-heroin-availability-feedback.txt
